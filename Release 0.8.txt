Today I am super-excited about my own software release. I'm the only developer, and the only user, but I've written some new features that I am very keen to start using. 
I mention this in case you were wondering exactly how nerdy and odd I am.

I don't know how to explain this concisely. I have a very large "universal playlist" where I gather all my entertainment. TV shows, books, movies, videos, web articles, and games. Everything goes on the list so I don't forget it. Because it's massive (currently over 16,000 items and growing) I will never get through it all. Choosing something to do (read, watch or play) next from a list that big is basically impossible. If I went chronologically, I'd just get further behind. So I have a voting system where randomly-paired items face off together. Whichever one appeals to me more is set forever as a higher priority than the other one. It took a long time to get here, but now I have one big tree of carefully-voted items, and all the stuff at the top is either my most-anticipated gold or new enough not to have disappeared yet. This works surprisingly well, but there's still a problem.
The items that have been on the list the longest are now down so deep that I might never get to them. Something like 42 levels down. With the way the voting works, they're likely to stay there pretty much forever, just shuffling up and down a level at a time as their distant, more popular "parent" items are voted on. But the deep tree has value, too. I added those items because, at some point, I wanted to read or watch or play them. And sometimes the newer items don't lead to much progress at all. When I mark them off, they don't show me new stuff underneath because they haven't been voted on much. What I wanted was a way to re-balance the tree so that the deep stuff gets shallower and the sparse parts get more full, while the most-upvoted items shed a few of their children.
This is what I wrote and am starting to release today, if the testing goes well. A feature that automatically rearranges the tree, compressing it vertically and evening out the number of children every item has. I expect it to change the way I manage my universal playlist as much as the voting process changed it last time. It's the big step I needed in order to manage this much data.
